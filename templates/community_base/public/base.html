Shared public templates in community-base extend "community_base/public/base.html" and fill five blocks: content, title, meta_description, page_head_metadata and extra_js. A site whose own base names all five needs no override. This site's templates/base.html names three of them the same way (title, meta_description, page_head_metadata) but calls its body slot "body" and its script slot "extra_scripts". Django resolves blocks by name across the whole inheritance chain rather than by lexical nesting, so wrapping the contracted name in the site name above is what makes content and extra_js reachable from a package page template. Only those two are remapped; remapping the other three would be a no-op at best and would shadow the site defaults at worst -- and Django rejects a template that nests a same-named block inside its own redefinition, so it is not even expressible. Without this file, Django drops content and extra_js in silence: the mounted public unsubscribe page returns 200 with ~21kB of site chrome and no form at all. Chrome. templates/base.html deliberately renders no header and no footer -- every page on this site supplies its own inside its own "body" block override. A seam that mapped content straight onto body would therefore hand every shared public page a navless, unbranded, container-less page.
